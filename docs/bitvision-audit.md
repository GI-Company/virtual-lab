# BitVision simulator audit in VirtualLab

The Computational Biology tab includes **BitVision audit**. Open a project experiment, enter absolute paths to the exported evaluation ZIP and both ONNX files, and run the local audit. The result is registered with the active experiment, appears in Computational Biology history and Projects, and can be exported in a `.vlab-study` file. The same audit is available through Python with `BitVisionAuditRequest`, `adapter_for`, `execute`, and `register`; see the reproduction script in the delivery package.

The worker reads the ZIP's episode records, summary, paired deltas, and action diagnostics without extracting the archive or deserializing the PyTorch checkpoint. It verifies same-seed checkpoint/untreated pairs, recomputes group outcomes and reported deltas, records any episodes marked both eradicated and failed, and flags near-saturated normalized dose components. It fingerprints both ONNX files and inspects their static input/output signatures and initializer dtypes. It does **not** execute the ONNX graphs or verify their full semantics. The large source ZIP and ONNX files are referenced by SHA256 and path; the portable study embeds the derived audit artifact, not copies of the source models or checkpoint.

The result has epistemic state `CALCULATED`, derived from source state `SIMULATED`, and status `SIMULATOR_ONLY`. Its episode outcomes are not biological measurements or treatment advice. Held-out UniProt accessions are still within the same simulator. AlphaFold CIF files in the archive are reference structures, not validation of the simulator's disease dynamics or actions. The current archive has no simulator implementation, cell-feature definitions and normalization, context feature definitions, six action meanings/units, reward specification, or active-treatment baseline. Those are necessary before policy replay or serious efficacy claims.

For the supplied checkpoint-500 export, the audit found 192 episodes in 96 same-seed pairs. Four held-out accessions had checkpoint eradication in 32/32 normal episodes and 14/32 hard episodes; the untreated comparator had 0/32 in both. Held-out hard mean toxicity was 4.974 normalized simulator units. Two training/hard episodes were marked both eradicated and failed. Some action components have mean normalized dose above 0.9. These are simulator findings only. Untreated is a weak baseline, and eight episodes per accession/regime/variant do not establish robust generalization.

The next experiment should freeze this policy and replay it on new simulator seeds with a simple **active** policy comparator, fixed action semantics, toxicity constraints, and prespecified endpoints. Then seek a separately acquired measured dataset for one narrow biological prediction. Longer training should follow evidence that the benchmark and model interface are meaningful, rather than replace that evidence.

The audit needs no network or API key. It limits the ZIP inventory and uncompressed size, reads selected metadata members of at most 10 MB, caps ONNX files at 64 MB, rejects mismatched episode pairs and summary/delta tables, and keeps checkpoint pickle bytes unopened. The protobuf signature reader is intentionally narrow and is not a replacement for the ONNX checker. The GUI worker is cancellable; incomplete runs are not registered. Portable studies verify artifact bytes, not author identity or biological validity.
